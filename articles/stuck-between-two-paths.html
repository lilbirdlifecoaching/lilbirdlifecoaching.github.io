Stuck between two paths? find the one that's yours.

By Luke Haythorpe · 6 min read

Two roads. You can see both of them clearly, and you've been standing at the fork for weeks, maybe months, unable to step onto either. The pros and cons are evenly matched, which is exactly why the list isn't helping. When two options look equal on paper, more paper won't settle it.

Being stuck between two paths is its own kind of stuck. You have options and you can see the way forward. Two ways, in fact, and something in you won't commit to either. That hesitation is worth understanding, because it usually points at the answer.

Why the pros and cons list failed you

A pros and cons list treats a decision like a math problem: tally the points, higher score wins. But big life decisions are about values, identity, and the kind of person you want to be. Those don't fit in a column.

When two paths score evenly, it usually means each is strong in a different way that matters to a different part of you. One meets your need for security, the other your need for meaning. The list can't settle that, because the real question is which part of you gets to lead.

When two paths look equal on paper, what you're really deciding is which part of yourself to trust.

Three questions that go deeper than pros and cons

1. Which path are you drawn to, and which are you running to?

Moving toward something is different from moving away from something. One path might really pull at you. The other might just be an escape from discomfort. Be clear with yourself about which is which. Decisions made while running away tend to recreate the same problem somewhere new.

2. Which choice are you avoiding because it's scarier?

Often you already know which path is yours, and you're stuck because it's the harder, more exposed one. What looks like indecision is often hesitating to make the brave choice. Ask yourself straight out: if fear weren't a factor, which would I pick? How fast you answer tells you a lot.

3. Which path would make a story you're glad to tell in ten years?

Picture yourself a decade from now, looking back. Which choice turns into a story you're glad you lived? Which one, if you skipped it, turns into a "what if" you carry around? Regret is a better guide than fear, and this question brings it up fast.

About the "wrong" choice

Part of what freezes people is the belief that one path is right, one is wrong, and choosing wrong would be a disaster. Let go of that a bit. Most of the time there isn't one correct path. There are different good lives down different roads, and a lot of what makes a choice right is the commitment you bring to it afterwards.

You can make either path work. What you can't do is keep standing at the fork. The weeks and months of being frozen, and the energy it takes to keep the question open, almost always cost more than choosing slightly wrong and adjusting.

When clarity won't come on your own

If you've worked through these questions and you're still stuck, you haven't failed. You're probably just too close to it. Fear, desire and other people's expectations are all talking at once, and it's hard to tell which voice is yours.

This is one of the best uses of a single coaching conversation. I won't tell you which road to take. I'll help you hear which one you already lean toward underneath all the noise. People often come into these conversations frozen between two paths and leave an hour later clear on what they want. They didn't get new information. They finally heard themselves think.

Standing between two paths feels cautious, but after a while it's a cost you keep paying. More list-making rarely helps. What helps is working out which path is desire and which is escape, which is brave and which is safe, and then trusting yourself enough to step forward. Either road can lead to a good life. Standing still won't.
